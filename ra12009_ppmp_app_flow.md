# PPMP to APP Consolidation Process under RA 12009 (NGPA)

The **Project Procurement Management Plan (PPMP)** to **Annual Procurement Plan (APP)** consolidation process under **Republic Act No. 12009** (also known as the **New Government Procurement Act or NGPA**, signed in July 2024 to replace/amend RA 9184) introduces several strategic changes. The law places a stronger emphasis on strategic planning, market scoping, fit-for-purpose procurement, and digital transformation.

---

## 1. The PPMP to APP Process Flow

### Phase 1: Strategic Planning & Market Scoping (End-User / PMO)
Before writing down numbers, End-User Units or Project Management Offices (PMOs) must conduct strategic planning:
* **Market Scoping:** End-users are now mandated to perform market research and cost estimation, evaluating supply positioning, industry lead times, storage requirements, and life-cycle costs.
* **Fit-for-Purpose Specifications:** Draft specifications, terms of reference (TOR), or scopes of work (SOW) are prepared to ensure the procurement achieves value for money.

### Phase 2: Drafting the Indicative PPMP (End-User / PMO)
* During the budget preparation stage (usually early in the preceding year), end-users prepare their **Indicative PPMP** for their respective Programs, Activities, and Projects (PAPs).
* **Key Fields in the PPMP:**
  * Project name, objectives, and type (Goods, Infrastructure, or Consulting Services).
  * Quantity, unit of measure, and estimated budget (Approved Budget for the Contract or ABC).
  * Procurement methods/modes (e.g., competitive bidding, alternative modes).
  * Time schedule/milestones for each procurement activity (advertisement, submission, evaluation, award).
  * Technical specifications/TOR.

### Phase 3: Submission & First Consolidation (BAC Secretariat)
* The PMOs and End-Users submit their Indicative PPMPs to the **Bids and Awards Committee (BAC) Secretariat**.
* The BAC Secretariat compiles and consolidates these individual PPMPs into the **Indicative APP**.
* The Indicative APP is reviewed to ensure it aligns with the agency’s budget proposal (e.g., National Expenditure Program or NEP for national agencies).

### Phase 4: Finalizing the PPMP & APP (Post-Budget Approval)
* Once the official budget allocation is approved (e.g., passage of the General Appropriations Act (GAA), corporate budget approval, or local appropriation ordinance):
  1. **PPMP Adjustment:** End-users adjust and finalize their PPMPs to match the actual approved budget.
  2. **Final Consolidation:** The BAC Secretariat consolidates the finalized PPMPs into the final **Annual Procurement Plan (APP)**.
  3. **Verification:** The BAC verifies that all items in the APP are consistent with the approved budget and are crucial to the agency’s operations.

### Phase 5: Approval and Publication (HoPE)
* The consolidated APP is submitted to the **Head of the Procuring Entity (HoPE)** (or their designated official) for formal approval.
* **Strict Enforcement:** Under the NGPA, no procurement can be legally undertaken unless it is included in an approved APP.
* **Publication:** The approved APP is uploaded to the agency's Transparency Seal and integrated with the modernized PhilGEPS (mPhilGEPS) platform.

---

## 2. Key Modernizations in RA 12009 Affecting Consolidation

* **Digital Integration:** The NGPA mandates the end-to-end digitalization of procurement. Consolidation processes will transition to utilizing unified electronic forms and templates directly integrated within the modern PhilGEPS portal.
* **Life-Cycle Costing:** Procuring entities must look beyond the lowest bid price and plan for operating, maintenance, and disposal costs within their PPMPs.
* **Transitory Period:** The GPPB provides standardized templates and forms to ensure consistency across all government agencies, with a general three-year transition period for full compliance.
